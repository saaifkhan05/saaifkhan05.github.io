KDR Generator

AI-powered close plans · NYS OGS · Jun – Aug 2026
The problem

Sellers were spending up to three hours assembling a single Key Deal Review deck before every cadence meeting — almost all of it manual. Nobody asked me to fix it. I watched it happen twice, then built the thing that fixed it.

What changed

Raw seller notes go in; a complete 12-stage close plan comes out in under thirty seconds. Built for the IBM watsonx Challenge on a team with a full-time technical seller and a managing director for the State of New York — I led it and built it myself.

Architecture

Four AI workflows underneath: structured deal extraction, skeleton inference, conversational plan patching, and deal narrative generation. Each emits strict JSON with a regex fallback, so a malformed model response degrades instead of taking production down.

Constraints that shaped it

Real-time validation against a 5,000+ SKU IBM OGS pricelist, @ibm.com authentication, and milestone-tagged routing that pushes email and in-app notifications to whoever owns the next step.

What I'd do differently

Ship the eval harness first. I tuned prompts on instinct for two weeks before I had any way to measure whether a change made output better or worse.

Retrieval over fine-tuning — the deal corpus changed weekly, and retraining cost more than it returned.

How it behaves. One toggle, top right. It swaps the body of every dossier — roles and projects — between the two lenses and remembers the choice in localStorage, so a hiring manager who picks Technical stays in Technical for the rest of the visit. Headline, company mark, and metrics never move: only the depth changes. Story is always the default, because most visitors are not engineers.
Why it is worth building. Right now both audiences read the same blend and neither gets their version. This is the cheapest way to serve both without maintaining two sites.
Cost. Roughly one extra content block per dossier — the parts that are already in your head but not on the page: stack, the decision you made, and the honest retrospective.
